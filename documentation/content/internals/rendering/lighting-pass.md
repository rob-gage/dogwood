`SceneRendererLightingPass` computes one light texture per rendered view over the whole resident cellular region, at the resolution selected by `LightingQuality`: one texel per 4 × 4 cells at Low, 2 × 2 at Medium, or one cell at High. The domain origin is aligned to world cell coordinates at that scale, and appearance shaders linearly sample the texel corresponding to each cell. High is the default and retains one texel per cell. The scene renderer runs it before fluid shading and the main render pass. Public radiance data and scene controls are described on [Lighting](@/external-api/lighting.md).

Actor and backlight rasters cover the same world area at every quality. Each optical texel averages straight paths across its rows and columns: radiance is composited front to back and transmission multiplied along each path. The mean path transmission becomes extinction as `-ln(mean transmission)`; emission is scaled so a ray crossing the texel gathers the mean path radiance. Uniform texels retain their world-unit behavior, while small solid objects retain their cross-section. Backlight has one texel per 4 × 4 world cells at every quality. Gameplay sprites are sampled at 2 × 2 (High) or 4 × 4 (Medium and Low) positions per lighting texel with mip selection; light discs use analytic texel coverage normalized to preserve total energy.

## Stages

1. Actor optics raster adds gameplay sprite emission multiplied by sprite tint and intensity and multiplies transmission for each covered cell. `ActorLightComponent` adds an analytically covered disc emitter. Shaded bodies and glowing decorations are drawn into this same raster inside the optical render pass after sprites and lights; details are on [scene rendering](@/internals/rendering/scene-renderer.md) and [chunk decorations](@/internals/rendering/decorations.md).
2. Backlight raster composites parallax backgrounds far to near with one texel per 4 × 4 world cells at every quality. Radiance-map mip level increases with physical depth.
3. Optical-field construction combines cellular and fluid radiance, gas species and atmospheres, actor optics, amount transforms, and temperature transforms. Cellular and fluid amount transforms are applied before temperature transforms. Per-cell extinction is `-ln(1 - occlusion)` per tile, divided by cells per tile and capped at `30`.
4. An 8 × 8 workgroup classifies uniform optical blocks; cascade rays cross a uniform block in one step. Radiance cascades otherwise trace rays with grid DDA and Beer-Lambert transmission. Level `i` probes every `2^i` lighting texels, uses `LIGHTING_DIRECTIONS * 2^i * texel_size` directions, and covers `[2(2^i - 1), 2(2^(i+1) - 1)]` lighting texels. This keeps ray density per world distance the same at each quality. Levels extend until the domain diagonal is covered. The coarsest level runs first; each interval combines its trace with rays from surrounding coarser probes. A ray is used per coarser probe only when its plain ray crosses matter or light; otherwise the plain ray and interpolation suffice. The plain ray stops as soon as it gathers light or loses transmission, since then only the per-probe rays are used. Exactly zero-emission, zero-extinction samples advance the ray without exponential or path-length work. The finest interval is integrated at every lighting texel into fluence rather than stored as a level. A level with fewer than `2^20` intervals on a discrete GPU, or `2^15` on any other, splits each ray into equal segments across up to 16 invocations, which compose their radiance and transmission in order through workgroup memory. For one-invocation rays, a separate barrier-free path skips unchanged intervals and coarser probes whose interpolation weight is zero. Every stored level keeps its intervals between frames; see the cache below.
5. Three separable spread passes (rows, columns, rows) move light into sufficiently solid cells. Attenuation is `smoothstep(SPREAD_THIN, SPREAD_SOLID, extinction per cell) * exp(-extinction / penetration)`, with `SPREAD_THIN = 0.01` and `SPREAD_SOLID = 0.06`. Thin matter such as water and smoke therefore does not take spreading light. Each line is scanned in both directions by one workgroup holding eight cells per invocation. A line that fits one segment keeps its cells between the forward and reverse scans; the reverse scan maps invocations from the far end of the line, and each cell is written once. Prefix-scan rounds alternate between two halves of workgroup memory, so each round needs only one barrier between reading its source and writing its destination. The final pass writes only the light texture, skipping its unread buffer store. Longer lines scan each direction separately. Spread buffers pack RGB as two `pack2x16float` words. Backlight enters the first pass and lights portions not hidden by a tile thickness.
6. Box-downsampled light mips support appearance shading. Where a level has an odd size, the next level's last texel also averages the trailing texel.

The cache compares optical and backlight textures after packing their `Rgba16Float` values, so unchanged stored inputs retain the previous light field and zero the indirect dispatch counts. A changed resident world origin, penetration, or cellular tile size forces recomputation, and a new lighting-domain allocation is also marked fresh. `SceneRendererLightingPass::compute` accepts a draw-optics closure, called after sprite and light optics in the actor optics render pass; it draws shaded bodies and glowing decorations into the same `Rgba16Float` target using `LIGHTING_OPTICS_BLEND`. Light sources decide which stages run:

- With neither optical nor backlight emission the light is exactly zero. The pass clears the light texture to `(0, 0, 0, 1)` and rebuilds its mips only on a fresh domain or when the last frame had a source; otherwise it keeps the zero light even across geometry and origin changes.
- With backlight but no optical emission, changed inputs skip block classification, cascade tracing, and integration, clear fluence, then run spread with backlight and rebuild light mips.
- With optical emission, changed inputs run the cascade path incrementally.

The incremental cascades keep one persistent interval buffer per level above the finest. The upper 16 bits of an interval's blue word, which radiance unpacking ignores, flag that its radiance changed this frame. An interval is recomputed when all intervals are forced, when the bounds of the changed optical cells, extended by nine cells, intersect the cells its rays and coarser probes can reach, or when a coarser interval it merges with changed. Otherwise it keeps its radiance and clears its flag. A summarize pass folds the flags of the second level into its first interval of each probe, and integration keeps a cell's previous fluence unless those flags, the changed bounds, or the force flag apply to it. The extra cells cover an off-ray change in a touched 8 × 8 block that can change how a ray groups its steps,. All intervals are forced for a changed key and when optical emission returns after a frame without it, since the skipped levels hold stale data.

For a normal appearance pass, lit color is `albedo * (ambient + light) + emission`. Pressure and temperature view tints and overlays remain unlit. With no resident region, appearance uses ambient only. Parallax planes sample by `log2(depth in cells)` and scale gameplay light by the fraction of the emitter kernel inside the domain.

## Resources and layouts

The optical field, actor optics, and light textures use `Rgba16Float`; the backlight texture has one texel per 4 × 4 world cells. Temperature keys arrive in `SceneGraphicsLighting.temperature_color_buffer`; its records have a `vec4<u32>` header per thermal material followed by eleven `vec4<f32>` values per key. `SceneGraphicsLighting` also carries ambient, penetration, `LightingQuality`, thermal offsets, and collected lights. Sprite instances carry an 80-byte record: radiance region starts at byte 56 and intensity at byte 72. `SceneGraphicsSprites` entries contain 12 values, including separate albedo and radiance views, independent regions, color, and intensity. Parallax records carry `radiance_texture_region` and `radiance_intensity` alongside their radiance view. The lighting raster uses a 64-byte instance stride and uses `vertex_covering` for sprites and lights so raster coverage reaches touched texels. The spread uniform's final word is the texel size in cells.

Appearance bind groups expose a view uniform, the sampled `Rgba16Float` light texture, and its filtering sampler; cellular, fluid, gas, and sprite passes each append this lighting group after their local bindings. Optical construction reads cell/material/gas/fluid/temperature data plus actor optics and writes an `Rgba16Float` field. Cascade dispatches bind each level's own interval buffer, the next coarser level's buffer, and the cache state buffer with per-level dynamic uniforms; spread dispatches use three ping-pong storage-texture bindings and dynamic uniforms; mip dispatches bind adjacent source and destination mip views.

The cache stores packed optical and backlight pixels per lighting domain and builds indirect dispatch arguments on the GPU. Its state buffer holds ten `u32` words: changed-input flags (optical and backlight), has optical emission, force all intervals, had optical emission, the four changed optical bounds, has backlight emission, and had any source. Each indirect record carries a kind that selects when it runs: spread, cascade, clear fluence, clear light, or mips. Spread selects 32, 64, 128, or 256 invocations for one-segment lines up to 2048 cells; longer lines use 256. Each invocation continues to process eight cells.

The pass uses a 256-byte dynamic-uniform stride for cascade and spread records. Cascade uniforms are allocated for up to 16 levels. The private tuning constants are `LIGHTING_INTERVAL = 2`, `LIGHTING_DIRECTIONS = 4`, `LIGHTING_BACKLIGHT_CELLS = 4`, and `LIGHTING_LEVEL_MAXIMUM = 16`. The shared radiance WGSL packs three 7-bit mantissas, a shared 4-bit exponent, and 7-bit occlusion into a `u32`; see [resource constants](@/architecture/resources-and-lifetimes.md).

> **Limitation:** There is no multiple-bounce reflection; spread is the only indirect term. Penetration follows rows and columns and is slightly shorter diagonally. Only the resident domain contributes, so distant lights do not reach the view. Parallax transport is approximate and backgrounds cover only visible copies.

At Low and Medium quality the pass keeps the previous light for up to 2 (Low) or 1 (Medium) consecutive frames (`LIGHTING_THROTTLE_FRAMES_LOW`, `LIGHTING_THROTTLE_FRAMES_MEDIUM`): `prepare` compares the world origin, domain size, penetration, texel size, and cellular tile size with those of the last computed frame, and `compute` returns before recording any lighting work only when they match, the domain is not fresh, and the streak limit is not reached. The optical field and cache are not touched on a kept frame, so the next computed frame compares against the last computed inputs and traces every change made meanwhile; computed frames therefore equal an unthrottled pass exactly, and a kept frame only lags the light without moving it. High never keeps light. Set `DOGWOOD_LIGHTING_THROTTLE=<frames>` to override the limit for both qualities, with `0` computing every frame.

Optical changes also populate an 8 × 8 block map. Two GPU scans build a summed-area table with a zero border. Cascade dependency rectangles first reject the global changed bounds, then query this table to avoid retracing intervals between separated local changes. Queries include the existing trace margin and an extra texel border; coarser interval change flags still propagate independently. The table occupies `(ceil(width / 8) + 1) * (ceil(height / 8) + 1)` words per domain. Set `DOGWOOD_LIGHTING_GLOBAL_DIRTY=1` to compare against the original global-bounds invalidation.

Lighting cache pipeline and bind-group layouts are reused from the previous domain when
quality or domain dimensions change. The new domain allocates its textures, buffers, and bind
groups, while retaining the already compiled cache pipelines. Moving the resident origin does
not recreate the domain. Resource allocation and GPU execution still occur at runtime.
